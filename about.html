About

Ink first, brief second.

Background, approach and the clients who've trusted the studio with their identities.

I started out painting signage for a jazz bar that couldn't afford a real designer, and never really left that palette. These days the clients are bigger — magazines, hospitality brands, the occasional record label — but the job hasn't changed: make something warm enough that people stop for a second.

The work is quiet by intention. Few elements, materials that age well, and typography doing most of the talking. I research before I sketch, and I cut decoration that doesn't earn its place. Most projects begin in gouache or risograph and end in vector for production.

I studied illustration at Cooper Union and spent four years in the art department at a culture magazine before opening the studio in 2019. I work from a shared floor in Brooklyn with two bookbinders and a letterpress that mostly works.
